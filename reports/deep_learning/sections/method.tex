\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

To make the three-paradigm comparison meaningful we first walk through the
architecture of each evaluated model: what is in the network, why each component
matters, and what it ultimately buys at evaluation time. The CNN paradigm we
\emph{reimplement} (Zhang~2016) is described in the most detail
(Sec.~\ref{sec:method:zhang16}); the two pretrained systems
(Secs.~\ref{sec:method:zhang17}--\ref{sec:method:deoldify}) are described at
the level needed to interpret their results in Sec.~\ref{sec:results}. A summary
view of the colorization data flow common to all three (luminance in, $ab$ out,
recombine into Lab, convert to RGB) is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:dl-pipeline}.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{Deep_Learning_method.png}
  \caption{Common Lab pipeline shared by all three benchmarked paradigms. The
  grayscale input provides the luminance $L$; the deep model predicts the
  chrominance $(a,b)$; prediction and input are recombined and converted to
  RGB. Models differ only in how the central $L\!\to\!(a,b)$ mapping is
  parameterized and trained.}
  \label{fig:dl-pipeline}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Common color representation}
All three models operate in CIE~Lab. The luminance channel $L\in[0,100]$ is
the network input (normalized to $[-1,1]$); the chrominance channels
$(a,b)\in[-110,110]$ are the prediction target. Lab linearly separates lightness
from color, so the model never has to reproduce the structure already present
in the input---it predicts only chrominance, which is then recombined with the
original $L$ and converted back to RGB for display and metrics. This Lab
factorization is what lets every paradigm below use a single-channel input and
a two-channel output, regardless of whether the network is a classification
CNN or a U-Net GAN.

\subsection{Paradigm 1 (reimplemented): Zhang 2016 — classification CNN}
\label{sec:method:zhang16}

Zhang \emph{et al.}~\cite{zhang2016colorful} were the first to observe that
treating colorization as $\ell_2$ regression \emph{collapses} multimodal targets
to their mean, producing the desaturated, sepia-tinted outputs of earlier
methods. Their fix has three coupled parts---a fully-convolutional dilated
encoder, a per-pixel classification head over a quantized $ab$ palette, and a
temperature-controlled \emph{annealed-mean} decoder---which we reimplement
faithfully.

\subsubsection{Network architecture}
The network (Fig.~\ref{fig:zhang16-arch}) is a fully-convolutional encoder of
eight blocks (\texttt{conv1}--\texttt{conv8}), 31.6\,M parameters. Three design
choices matter:

\paragraph{No pooling, only stride-2 convolutions.}
Blocks 1--3 downsample $256\!\times\!256$ to $32\!\times\!32$ at $64/128/256$
channels by stride-2 convolutions instead of max-pool. Strided convolutions are
learnable, so the network chooses which low-level features to keep at each
downsampling step rather than pooling them blindly; this preserves chrominance
cues that pooling tends to wash out.

\paragraph{Dilated convolutions in the middle blocks.}
Blocks 4--7 hold spatial resolution at $32\!\times\!32$ with $512$ channels and
introduce dilation $d=2$ in blocks 5--6. Dilations expand the effective
receptive field exponentially without adding parameters or further
downsampling. A wide receptive field is what lets the network infer
\emph{semantically} consistent colors: the model needs to ``see'' enough of a
region to decide ``this is grass'' versus ``this is foliage,'' and dilation buys
that context cheaply.

\paragraph{$1\!\times\!1$ classification head and bilinear upsampling.}
Block 8 upsamples once via a transposed convolution and ends in a
$1\!\times\!1$ convolution producing $Q$ logits per spatial location at
$64\!\times\!64$; the predicted $ab$ map is bilinearly upsampled to full
resolution at the very end. The network therefore reasons about color at a
coarse, semantic resolution and lets the input $L$ carry the high-frequency
structure---an efficient division of labor that keeps the model small.

Every convolution is followed by ReLU and each block by BatchNorm; both
contribute to training stability rather than to the colorization recipe per se.

  31.6\,M~params). Stride-2 convolutions downsample to $32\!\times\!32$

\subsubsection{Quantized $ab$ palette}
The output side is what makes Zhang 2016 distinct. Instead of regressing
$(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^2$, the $ab$ plane is discretized on a $10\!\times\!10$
grid and restricted to bins that map to valid sRGB colors at some lightness,
yielding $Q\!=\!233$ in-gamut bins (Fig.~\ref{fig:abquant}). (The official Zhang
implementation ships $313$ bins from a different gamut hull; our
independently-computed grid yields $233$, a detail that matters in
Sec.~\ref{sec:results}.) Ground-truth chrominance is \emph{soft-encoded}: each
pixel's $(a,b)$ is assigned to its $5$ nearest bins with Gaussian weights
($\sigma=5$), giving a target distribution $Z\in[0,1]^{H\!\times\!W\!\times\!Q}$
rather than a one-hot label. Soft encoding makes the loss surface smooth and
lets the network express \emph{uncertainty} over plausible colors instead of
having to commit to one.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.78\columnwidth]{ab_quantization.png}
  \caption{The $ab$ color space quantized into $233$ in-gamut bins (shown at
  $L^{*}=70$). The network predicts a categorical distribution over these bins
  rather than a continuous $(a,b)$ pair, which is the mechanism that preserves
  color multimodality.}
  \label{fig:abquant}
\end{figure}

\subsubsection{Class-rebalanced classification loss}
Let $\widehat{Z}=\mathcal{G}(L)$ be the predicted per-pixel distribution. We
minimize a multinomial cross-entropy between $\widehat{Z}$ and the soft target
$Z$, reweighted to counter the strong prior toward desaturated colors:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\widehat{Z}, Z) =
  -\sum_{h,w} v\!\left(Z_{h,w}\right)
  \sum_{q=1}^{Q} Z_{h,w,q}\,\log \widehat{Z}_{h,w,q}.
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
The pixel weight depends on the dominant ground-truth bin
$q^{\star}=\arg\max_q Z_{h,w,q}$ through a rebalancing factor
\begin{equation}
  v(Z_{h,w}) = w_{q^{\star}},\qquad
  w \;\propto\; \Big((1-\lambda)\,\widetilde{p} + \tfrac{\lambda}{Q}\Big)^{-1},
  \label{eq:rebalance}
\end{equation}
where $\widetilde{p}$ is the smoothed empirical bin frequency over the training
set, $\lambda=0.5$ mixes it with a uniform prior, and $w$ is normalized so that
$\mathbb{E}[w]=1$. Rare, vivid colors thus receive larger gradients than the
ubiquitous low-saturation background---this is what prevents the network from
collapsing onto the easy ``everything brown/gray'' solution.

\subsubsection{Annealed-mean decoding}
At inference the per-pixel distribution must be collapsed to a single $(a,b)$.
Taking the mode is faithful but spatially inconsistent; taking the mean
desaturates. Following~\cite{zhang2016colorful} we use the \emph{annealed mean}
with temperature $T$:
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{ab}_{h,w} = \sum_{q=1}^{Q} f_T\!\left(\widehat{Z}_{h,w}\right)_q\, c_q,
  \quad
  f_T(\widehat{Z})_q = \frac{\exp\!\big(\log \widehat{Z}_q / T\big)}
                            {\sum_{q'} \exp\!\big(\log \widehat{Z}_{q'} / T\big)},
  \label{eq:annealed}
\end{equation}
where $c_q$ is the center of bin $q$. We use $T=0.38$, which interpolates
between the over-saturated mode ($T\!\to\!0$) and the washed-out mean
($T\!=\!1$). The decoded $ab$ map is bilinearly upsampled to full resolution
and recombined with the input $L$. This single hyperparameter is what trades
saturation against spatial coherence in the final output.

\subsection{Paradigm 2: Zhang 2017 — interactive CNN (automatic mode)}
\label{sec:method:zhang17}

The follow-up of Zhang \emph{et al.}~\cite{zhang2017realtime} attacks a
different problem: \emph{interactive} colorization, in which the user can paint
sparse color hints onto a grayscale image and see them propagated globally in
real time. The architecture extends the Zhang~2016 backbone with a second input
branch and U-shape skip connections (Fig.~\ref{fig:zhang17-arch}); we evaluate
it in its publicly-released ``automatic mode,'' i.e.\ with all hints set to
zero, to compare it on identical terms to the other paradigms.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{zhang17_arch.png}
  \caption{Zhang 2017 interactive CNN. The main path is a U-Net-style encoder--bottleneck--decoder
  on the luminance channel with symmetric skip connections (purple). A second
  branch encodes sparse user-supplied $(a,b)$ hints together with a binary mask
  channel and concatenates them at the bottleneck. In our \emph{automatic-mode}
  evaluation the hint inputs are all zeros; the network is therefore asked to
  colorize from luminance alone---the regime it was \emph{not} optimized for.
  Schematic adapted from \cite{zhang2017realtime}.}
  \label{fig:zhang17-arch}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{What each branch contributes.}
The luminance branch shares Zhang~2016's encoder/dilated bottleneck/decoder
backbone but replaces the classification head with direct $ab$ regression and
adds explicit \emph{symmetric skip connections} (a true U-Net) so that
high-frequency edge structure flows from the encoder straight into the
decoder. The skip connections are crucial for hint propagation: a single
clicked color point must reach distant pixels along sharp edges, and skips
provide that path. The hint branch is a small CNN that turns the
$(a_{\text{hint}}, b_{\text{hint}}, \text{mask})$ image into a feature map that
is concatenated with the luminance bottleneck.

\paragraph{Why automatic mode underperforms.}
With zero hints, the hint branch contributes a tensor of zeros and the network
is reduced to a U-Net regressor on luminance with no class-rebalanced
classification objective and no annealed-mean decoder. The same training
objective that made the model excellent at \emph{following hints} (sharp
boundaries, faithful propagation) becomes its weakness in autonomous mode: it
defaults to under-saturated, sometimes blotchy chrominance because there is no
user signal indicating where to be bold. This explains the result reported in
Sec.~\ref{sec:results}, and is itself a finding---an interactive network is
the wrong tool for a hands-off pipeline.

\subsection{Paradigm 3: DeOldify — GAN (NoGAN recipe)}
\label{sec:method:deoldify}

DeOldify~\cite{antic2019deoldify} is the most widely-deployed practical
descendant of GAN-based colorization. There is no peer-reviewed paper, but the
recipe is well-documented and we summarize its three components: a U-Net
generator with self-attention, a PatchGAN critic, and the \emph{NoGAN}
training schedule (Fig.~\ref{fig:deoldify-arch}). Our evaluation uses the
``Artistic'' checkpoint exactly as released; we do not retrain.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{deoldify_arch.png}
  \caption{DeOldify generator and the NoGAN training schedule. The generator
  is a U-Net with a ResNet-34 encoder (ImageNet-initialized), self-attention
  at the bottleneck, and dense skip connections to the decoder. The critic is
  a spectral-normalized PatchGAN that compares full RGB outputs to real
  images. NoGAN pre-trains $G$ and $D$ separately, then runs a very short
  adversarial fine-tune, sidestepping the instability that has historically
  made colorization GANs hard to train.}
  \label{fig:deoldify-arch}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Why a ResNet-34 encoder.}
Initializing the encoder from an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-34 imports strong
mid-level features (textures, parts, object semantics) for free, which
dramatically reduces the data and time needed to learn ``what color this
\emph{thing} usually is.'' Skip connections then feed those semantic features
back into the decoder.

\paragraph{Why the self-attention bottleneck.}
A vanilla convolutional bottleneck can only relate spatially nearby features;
self-attention~\cite{vitoria2020chromagan} (independently and contemporaneously
adopted by ChromaGAN's spirit) lets a single airplane pixel attend to the
entire sky and adopt a globally-consistent color. This is the mechanism behind
DeOldify's confident, coherent saturation on large ambiguous regions, which we
see clearly in the qualitative results.

\paragraph{The PatchGAN critic and the NoGAN recipe.}
The critic is a spectral-normalized PatchGAN: instead of one global real/fake
score it returns a grid of patch-level scores, which encourages local realism
and avoids mode collapse to a uniformly-confident global color. NoGAN further
stabilizes training by (i)~pretraining $G$ to convergence with a perceptual
loss alone, (ii)~pretraining $D$ on $G$'s frozen outputs, then (iii)~running
adversarial training for only a small fraction of the total epochs. The
adversarial phase is brief enough that the critic never has time to overwhelm
the generator---historically the failure mode of colorization GANs. The
practical consequence is the deeply-saturated, restoration-grade output that
makes DeOldify the SOTA-class anchor of our comparison.

\subsection{Putting it together}
The three paradigms make different choices on a small number of axes that turn
out to determine their behavior:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Output parameterization.} Classification over a discrete palette
        (Zhang16) or continuous regression (Zhang17, DeOldify). Classification
        preserves multimodality explicitly; regression collapses it.
  \item \emph{Training objective.} Class-rebalanced cross-entropy (Zhang16),
        $\ell_2$/perceptual (Zhang17), perceptual + adversarial (DeOldify).
        Each objective biases the model toward a different point on the
        saturation--realism axis.
  \item \emph{Inductive priors.} Trained from scratch on COCO (Zhang16, ours),
        or initialized from large pretrained backbones (ImageNet ResNet for
        DeOldify). Bigger priors give better perceptual quality at higher
        latency and memory.
  \item \emph{Inference cost.} All three are single forward passes
        (${\sim}0.2$\,s per image on a 6\,GB GPU), so the speed--quality
        frontier between them is fairly tight.
\end{itemize}
Sections~\ref{sec:experiments} and \ref{sec:results} measure these design
choices against each other on a single benchmark.
