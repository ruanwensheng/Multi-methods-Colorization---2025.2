\title{Deep Learning Approaches to Image Colorization:\\
A Comparative Study of Four Paradigms on COCO 2017}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockA{Computer Vision Course Project\\
Deep Learning Method}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Automatic image colorization---predicting plausible chrominance for a grayscale
input---has been attacked by four successive deep-learning paradigms: feed-forward
CNNs, user-guided interactive CNNs, generative adversarial networks (GANs), and
diffusion models. We reimplement the seminal classification-based CNN of Zhang
\emph{et al.}~\cite{zhang2016colorful}, fine-tune it on COCO 2017, and benchmark
it against three representative pretrained systems---an interactive
CNN~\cite{zhang2017realtime} in automatic mode, the NoGAN
DeOldify~\cite{antic2019deoldify}, and a ControlNet~\cite{zhang2023controlnet}
diffusion pipeline---on a leakage-controlled, stratified 1{,}000-image subset of
the COCO 2017 \texttt{test2017} split. All numbers are reported as
\texttt{mean [95\% CI]} via a 10{,}000-resample percentile bootstrap. Our
fine-tuned model reaches PSNR $23.29$\,dB / SSIM $0.919$ / LPIPS $0.192$,
closing most of the gap to the SOTA-class GAN anchor ($24.00$\,dB) while running
${\sim}2.8\times$ faster. We document an honest reproducibility gap in the
diffusion slot, where the upstream Stable Diffusion 2.1 dependency has been
deprecated. The study highlights that, for fully-automatic colorization on
6\,GB-class hardware, a well-trained CNN remains a strong speed--quality
compromise.
\end{abstract}
